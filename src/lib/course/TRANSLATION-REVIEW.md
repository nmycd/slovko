# Uzbek and Georgian: questions for native reviewers

The Uzbek (Latin script) and Georgian versions were translated from the English by
AI (four translators per language in parallel), then made consistent by one reader
per language. Nobody who speaks either language has checked them yet. These are
the points the translators were least sure about; anything else that reads oddly
is just as welcome.

How to report: a screenshot, or the unit and lesson ("Unit 3 · Lesson 2") with the
wording you'd use instead. Settled wordings are listed in
[TRANSLATING.md](TRANSLATING.md) ("Settled renderings"); a change there applies
everywhere.

## Uzbek

1. **Level names**: Boshlangʻich / Elementar / Oʻrta / Oʻrtadan yuqori. Is
   "Elementar" right for A2 (or "Tayanch")?
2. **Interface words**: "Ketma-ket kunlar" for the day streak (or "Kunlik
   seriya"?); "hisob" for account (or "akkaunt"?); suffixes on brand names
   ("Slovkoni", "Premiumga oʻtish").
3. **Greetings**: Dobrý deň is "Assalomu alaykum", Dobrú chuť "Yoqimli ishtaha!"
   (or "Osh boʻlsin!"?). Polite forms are labelled "(siz)": clear, or was
   "(rasmiy)" better?
4. **"mehmonxona"** stands for both the living room (unit 3) and a hotel (units 4
   and 8). Confusing? ("yashash xonasi" / "zal" for the living room?)
5. **"boʻlim"** is both a course unit and a department at work (unit 8).
6. **Christmas** is "Rojdestvo". Or another word?
7. **Soup with "eat"**: "Kecha shoʻrva yegan" follows the Slovak verb jesť, though
   "shoʻrva ichmoq" is the usual phrase. Acceptable for teaching?
8. **The name Jana** is "Yana", which is also the word "again". Sentences write
   "Yana — oʻqituvchi." with a dash. Does it read clearly?
9. **Times**: 8:15 is "Soat sakkizdan chorak oʻtdi", and a card contrasts Slovak
   "štvrť na deväť" with Uzbek "toʻqqizga chorak qoldi". Correct?
10. **Grammar terms**: case names are given in Slovak with the Uzbek term in
    brackets (akuzatív (tushum kelishigi)). Is "infinitív" understandable, or
    should it be "harakat nomi"?

## Georgian

1. **"Unit" is თავი** ("chapter") throughout. Or ნაწილი / მოდული?
2. **Interface words**: the streak „დღე ზედიზედ“ / „დღეების სერია“; "native
   audio" „ენის მატარებლების გახმოვანებით“ (a calque?); endings hyphenated onto
   Latin words and brands (akuzatív-ში, Premium-ით).
3. **Case names**: Slovak name plus a Georgian term in brackets. ბრალდებითი
   (accusative) and ადგილობითი (locative) aren't Georgian cases: are they still
   understandable?
4. **The si card** (unit 9) compares Slovak *si* ("for yourself") with the
   Georgian ი- prefix („ვიყიდე მაისური“). Is the comparison right?
5. **volať sa** is glossed „რქმევა“ (a verbal noun, like the other infinitives).
   Clear, or is „ჰქვია“ better?
6. **Ordering food**: Dám si X is „X-ს ავიღებ“, also at a conference coffee break
   (unit 9), where „ყავას დავლევ“ might be more natural.
7. **mať rád about people** is „მომწონს“. Does it sound romantic? („მიყვარს“?)
8. **čerstvý** (fresh) and **nový** (new) are both „ახალი“.
9. **Floors** are counted the Georgian way: prvé poschodie = მეორე სართული, with a
   card in unit 8 explaining it. Right call?
10. **Times and tenses**: štvrť na deväť is „ცხრის თხუთმეტი წუთი“; „მაპატიეთ, რომ
    ვაგვიანებ“ (or „დავაგვიანე“?); Slovak imperfective past is sometimes given as a
    completed past (unit 10).
